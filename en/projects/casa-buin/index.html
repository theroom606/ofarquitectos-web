A house in a former avocado orchard.

Casa Buin
Buin, Chile
Private house / Built
Architecture
OF Arquitectos + Paula Velasco + Alberto Moletto
Year
2011
Photography
Marcos Mendizábal

Orientation, shade and even daylight.

Casa Buin stands in a former avocado orchard. Its most open façade faces north, while the entrance, kitchen and bathrooms are placed to the south. On the east side, the terrace sits beside an old avocado tree from the orchard, whose shade provides relief on warm summer days. Reclaimed timber wraps the walls and roof as a single folded skin.

01

North and east.

The north-facing opening connects the living spaces to the garden. The terrace occupies the eastern end of the house beside the existing avocado tree; its canopy offers a shaded place to be outdoors in summer.

02

Light from the south.

A corridor runs east to west between the bedrooms and service areas. A south-facing rooflight gives it even daylight throughout the day without exposing it directly to the stronger northern sun and heat.

03

A folded timber skin.

The cladding boards arrived by truck from a property near Pucón. Reclaimed from old timber barns in southern Chile, they cover both the walls and the roof. The same material wraps the volume like a continuous skin. With no eaves, the roof folds into several planes, giving the house a form closer to origami than to a conventional country house. Reusing these boards gives them another life as many timber barns are replaced by buildings clad in corrugated zinc sheets.
